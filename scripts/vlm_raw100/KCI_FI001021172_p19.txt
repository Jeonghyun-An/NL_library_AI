코메니우스 범교육론의 도덕적 속성에 관한 연구

<Abstract>

The research regarding a moral attribute which is immanent in comenius’s pampaedia

Lee, Sang-Hyun

John Amos Comenius Called the Father of Modern Education is also known as Jan Amos Komensky and was born on March 28, 1592 in Nivnice, Moravia and he died on November 15, 1670. From the persecution and hardships he suffered in his life, he came to develop a philosophy, called pansosophism(Pampaedia), which emphasized political unity, religious reconciliation, and cooperation in education. This philosophy of pansosophism related education to everyday life and called for a systematic relationship to be developed for all knowledge.

Arguing that Comenius’ ideas have been distorted by Marxist and pragmatist thinkers alike, the present work seeks to relocate them in the Judaeo-Christian traditions from which they sprang. It reexamines the principles of Comenius’ pedagogic philosophy, giving particular attention to the learner-centred methods of teaching which constitute his main legacy to world education.

He advocated the necessity of three kinds of education: intellectual education, moral education, and religious education. To teach ‘all things to all men’s was the theme of Comenius’ theory of education, which was called ‘Pampaedia’. Comenius considered that the talent to realize the goals of education is naturally inherent in people, and it is the role of education to bring out this natural gift, that is ‘nature’. Comenius said that, fundamentally, parents are responsible for education, but should they become unable to do it, schools would become necessary to replace them. According to Comenius, the image of the ideal person was that of a ‘pansophist,’ or a person who has learned all knowledge concerning God, nature, and human beings.

Key words: Comenius, Pampaedia, moral attribute, moral education
