In 2003, He Jiuying(何九盈) responding to these theoretical concerns, explicitly proposed the “Scattered-Point and Multi-Line”(散點多線式) model. According to this perspective, each historical stage of Chinese phonology exhibits synchronic dialectal variation, and even within the same period multiple phonological system-points may coexist, each following its own developmental trajectory. Phonological change, therefore, unfolds not along a single linear path but through a multilinear and structurally plural pattern of development.

This study reexamines the theoretical limitations inherent in the “Three-Point-One-Line” and “Nine-Point-One-Line” models and analyzes the methodological and historiographical issues raised by the adoption of the “Scattered-Point and Multi-Line” framework. In doing so, it argues for a necessary shift in the historiography of Chinese phonology—from a unilinear, lineage-centered narrative toward an approach grounded in multilinear developmental structures. Such a reorientation provides a more adequate conceptual foundation for understanding the spatial and temporal complexity of phonological change in the history of the Chinese language.
