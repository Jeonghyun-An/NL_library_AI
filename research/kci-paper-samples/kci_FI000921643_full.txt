Benefactive Verbal Constructions in Korean: A Functional Approach*

Seok-Hoon You (Korea University)

You, Seok-Hoon. Benefactive Verbal Constructions in Korean: A Functional Approach. Discourse and Cognition 10.2, 75-93. The so-called benefactive verbal construction (BVC hereafter) in the form of 'V + /e/a cwuta/tulita' is pervasive in Korean language and has drawn attentions from many researchers in the following terms: First, the unique underlying argument structure of the construction as a subtype of complex verbal constructions (CVC hereafter). Second, the rules and constraints involved in the realization of underlying argument structures to the nominals in the surface sentences. Third, the selectional restrictions involved in the selection of cooccurring verbs. Fourth, change of meaning due to grammaticalization from CVC's (donatory or transferentive meaning) to BVC's (benefactive meaning). In this paper, the unique properties and the licensing mechanism of the argument structure of BVC's are explored and two contrasting approaches will be reviewed: one is rather a formal one and the other one is a functional one. It will be suggested as a conclusion that a set of functional filters provide clearer explanation about the construction. (Korea University)

Key words: argument structure, benefactive verbal construction, functional approach, functional filters, grammaticalization, transferentive meaning

As in previous studies (Grimshaw and Mester 1988; Grimshaw 1990; Zaenen and Goldberg 1993; Chung 1993; You 1998), I assume that AS is the level of linguistic representation that mediates between lexical semantics and syntactic representation. In most current syntactic and morphological theories, it is generally accepted that every verbal element carries more or less fully specified AS in terms of thematic roles. On surface syntactic level, thematic roles are mapped onto grammatical relations such as Subject, Direct Object, Indirect Object, Complement, Locative, etc., on one hand, and onto Cases such as Nominative, Accusative, Dative, Locative, Genitive, etc. on the other by the application of relevant rules and principles. Arguments are realized on the surface as nominals (noun, pronoun, nominalized clause, etc.) typically followed by one or more overt or covert Case particles, and optionally flanked by one or more delimiter particles. Under the monoclausal approach, due to the single-predicate assumption, it is inevitable to introduce the notion of argument percolation or merger or identification or inheritance (Higginbotham 1985, Rosen 1989, and Li 1990) in order to decide the AS of complex predicates.
For example, viewing SVC's as compound verbs, Chung (1993) proposes the following for mantul-e cwuta 'to make to give'.

Suh (2000), adopting Jackendoff (1990, 1997), also follows monoclausal approach for a treatment of seemingly complicated Auxiliary Verbal Constructions (AVC's hereafter). He argues that an otherwise full lexical verb, once it takes part in the AVC, comes to exhibit drastically different syntactic and semantic properties from its lexical verb counterpart.
He assumes that such properties of the auxiliary verbs follow from the assumption that the AVC is a type of 'constructional idiom'4) listed in the lexicon. Thus, he claims that AUX-verbs do not have any AS. Argument Licensing

3.1 Argument Licensing for Grammaticality

In this paper, it is assumed that the licensing of argument NP's is tightly controlled by the EAS of the component verbals, and Korean BVC's are essentially biclausal.

Consequently, the manner of control is relevant to the biclausal nature of the construction.
It is proposed that the following conditions, parameter, and rule control the licensing of arguments in Korean BVC's to determine their grammaticality:

(7) a.
Subject Identity Condition (SIC): The first theta role (subject) of V1 and the first theta role (subject) of V2 are required to be referentially identical.
Object Identity Condition (OIC): The theme of V1 is referentially identical with the theme of V2. Exhaustive Discharge Condition (EDC): All theta roles in both AS's must be discharged to overt or covert clause-mate argument NP's exhaustively. Head-final Parameter (HFP): The theta roles of the head verb (V2) take precedence in realizing the arguments and Case marking them. Stray Argument Licensing Rule (SAL): V1 realizes only those of its arguments that are not referentially taken care of by the theta roles of V2. 3.2 Subject Choice Hierarchy and Grammatical Relations

It has been proposed in the literature that there is a universal hierarchy of theta roles (e.g., Bresnan and Kanerva 1989; Foley and van Valin 1984, Grimshaw 1990). Such theta role hierarchy is necessary, first of all, to explicate subject choice. For example, for a verbal with the <ag th go> theta-grid, <ag> is chosen as subject, and for a verbal with the <th go> theta-grid, <th> is chosen as subject, and so on following the order of theta roles within the hierarchy. I will simply assume the following as a basic theta role hierarchy for subject choice in Korean. (8) Subject Choice Hierarchy in Korean ag > ex > ins > go/so/di > th > co > lo

Thus, the nominal argument corresponding to the leftmost theta role of the verbal functions as the subject of the clause. When theme is not at the leftmost position, its corresponding nominal argument is the object of the clause. 4.1 Grammaticalization in -E cwuta and Valency Differences in AS

In this section, I claim that the constructions actually reflect different stages of an on-going gradual grammaticalization process along the cline from a full donatory or dative to a full benefactive construction.
I also claim that both structural and functional factors are deeply involved in the process. The grammaticalization has taken place only when cwuta occurred after a complementizer -E. Thus, -E cwuta has the original ungrammaticalized meaning 'and then give something to' as in (9a) and the grammaticalized benefactive meaning 'give the benefit/act of doing/being for' as in (9b). I thus claim that the donatory cwuta and the benefactive cwuta have different AS's as in the following:

(9) a. donatory cwuta: <ag go th> b. benefactive cwuta: <ag (go) th> [+CS]

Since BVC's are a subtype of AVC's, they share the following lexico-syntactic properties. (30) V2: cwuta 'give the benefit of doing to (someone)/to do/be for (someone)' [+ CM] <ag (go)> V1: Verb CM: -E

argument. Thus, the Object Identity Condition is vacuous. Notice in the above diagram that the embedded theme argument is realized according to the SAL rule because V2 does not contain its own theme. 5.1 Argument Licensing Mechanism in BVC's

Korean auxiliary verbs are a group of verbs that have undergone syntactic and semantic change due to grammaticalization processes. (Sohn 1994) Thus, they cannot stand alone without support from a preceding verb that is often called a main verb. Consider the examples below and compare the differences between the two in the manner of argument licensing:

(32) a.
John-i PROi phyenci-lul ttut-E po-ass-ta.
John-NM ag2 agl thl <ag1 thl>v1 <ag2>v2 (i) 'John opened and read the letter.' (ii) 'John tried opening the letter.'

b. Yumii-ka John-eykey PROi sakwa-lul sa-E cwu-ess-ta. Yumi-NM ag2 go2 agl thl <ag1 thl>v1 <ag2 thl go2>v2 (i) 'Yumi bought an apple and gave it to John.' (ii) 'Yumi bought an apple for John.'

I suggest that the grammaticality of the above and all other CVC's be licensed by a set of argument licensing conditions that were proposed in (7).
As already claimed, the so-called auxiliary benefactive verb cwuta 'to do V1 for ...', is required to have a complement clause.
Sentence (32a) satisfies the SIC in that the subject of the main clause John (<ag2>) and the embedded subject (PRO: <ag1>) are coreferential. The OIC is vacuously met because the auxiliary verb pota 'try to' has only <ag> as a theta role. The EDC is also met in that all the theta roles of both V1 and V2 are discharged, one to one, to all the overt or covert nominal arguments. The HFP is also satisfied because the argument with <ag2> is realized on the surface. Finally, the SAL is also observed because the embedded theme (<th1>) phyenci whose theta role is not specified in the EAS of V2 is realized on the surface. Thus, the sentence is licensed as grammatical.
Concluding Remarks

In this paper, I have discussed the BVC's in Korean as for their AS to see whether previous proposals about the AS of the BVC's adequately explain the ubiquitous nature of them.
It has been found that the AS proposed by previous researchers is not rich

enough to explain the behavior of the BVC's. The following are proposed to provide better explanation of the ubiquitous behavior of argument licensing in the BVC's in Korean: First, the enriched representation of the AS of BVC's; second, an extensive taxonomy of clause combining constructions in terms of syntactic and AS's; and third, five argument licensing conditions and their applications to different BVC's. I have also discussed the mapping of arguments into Cases (nominative, accusative, dative, locative, etc.) and grammatical relations (subject, direct or indirect object, complement, etc.).
