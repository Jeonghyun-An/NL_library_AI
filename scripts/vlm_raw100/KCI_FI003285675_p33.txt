Abstract

An Actor-Network Analysis of France’s 5G Policy Communication: The Policy Dilemma between Techno-nationalism and Technological Democracy

Jinrang Lee
(Hankuk University of Foreign Studies)

This study investigates the introduction of 5G in France through the analytical framework of Actor-Network Theory (ANT) in order to elucidate the democratic legitimacy of technology policy and the structural characteristics of policy communication. Despite France’s long-standing institutionalization of technological democracy since the 1990s, the rollout of 5G exposed persistent tensions among central authorities, local governments, civil society, and international actors—most notably the United States and China. Using government and regulatory documents, parliamentary debates, civil society publications, major media coverage, and lobbying records, this study reframes messages, messengers, and media as actors and reconstructs their interactions along ANT’s four stages of translation: problematisation, obligatory passage point, interessement/enrolment, and mobilisation.

The analysis demonstrates that France’s 5G policy communication is a multilayered political process reshaped by the collision of competing discourses: cybersecurity and techno-sovereignty narratives informed by global technological power rivalries; environmental and health risk frames advanced by civil society and municipal governments; conspiracy theories amplified through social media during the COVID-19 pandemic; and the politicization of scientific judgment generated by institutionalized deliberative procedures. Initially positioned as a strategic national technology through the government’s 5G roadmap, 5G became entangled in U.S.-China techno-geopolitical competition and confronted local moratorium movements, revealing a structural policy dilemma between techno-nationalism and technological democracy. Subsequent expansion of citizen deliberation at the municipal level strengthened the procedural legitimacy of the deployment process, yet simultaneously opened new arenas for policy contestation.
